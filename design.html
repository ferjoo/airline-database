Design document

Entity-relationship model.

The conceptual design behind the airline operations database: how it is drawn, the decision that shapes it, every entity and relationship, the attributes that need special treatment, the rules a diagram cannot hold, and the alternatives that were weighed.

1. Model summary

ConceptCount
Entities29
Relationships31
Weak entities7
Associative entities (N:N with attributes)6
Subtypes (specialization)2
Foreign keys36

The model was built in Oracle SQL Developer Data Modeler and is presented split into six thematic subviews, attached at the end of this document together with the complete diagram. The diagrams are vector graphics and can be enlarged without losing sharpness.

Why 36 foreign keys for 31 relationships. A relationship resolved through an associative entity is one relationship in the conceptual model but two foreign keys in the relational model, one toward each participant. Four of the relationships in section 4 are N:N through an associative entity (21, 22, 23 and 24) and one is the recursive connection between itinerary legs (26), so 31 relationships become 31 + 5 = 36 foreign keys.

2. The decision that orders the model

Whether a flight operation is identified by the flight number alone or by the number plus the date is the choice that defines the central structure of the model.

A flight number does not identify an operation. AA123 is a commercial product: the route the airline publishes and sells under the same number every day of the year. It cannot be the key of an operation, because AA123 on September 15 and AA123 on September 16 are different events, with a different crew, a different aircraft, different gates, different delays and a different operational status.

That is why the model separates three levels:

EntityPrimary keyWhat it represents
VUELOnum_vueloThe commercial product: the published route.
OPERACION_VUELOnum_vuelo + fecha_operacionThe instance of the flight on a specific date. Weak entity of VUELO: without the flight, the date identifies nothing.
SEGMENTOnum_vuelo + fecha_operacion + num_secuenciaEach leg between two airports within that operation. Weak entity of OPERACION_VUELO. num_secuencia preserves the order of the stops.

Without this split it would be impossible to record two operations of the same flight, to keep history, or to assign different crews and aircraft per day. In addition, having num_secuencia inside the key prevents more than one identical segment from existing within the same operation.

The model is separated from the aircraft because two different rules depend on it: crew qualifications are granted per model and not per registration, and the minimum runway length is a property of the model.

The seat hangs from the aircraft and not from the flight: a seat belongs to a specific aircraft and a specific class. Seat 14C is a physical property of the airplane. What changes from flight to flight is its occupancy, and that is resolved in RESERVA_SEGMENTO and INVENTARIO_PLAZAS.

INVENTARIO_PLAZAS is classified as associative because it relates each segment with each cabin class and carries the attributes of that pair (total, occupied and available seats). The class side is a closed domain rather than an entity of its own (see 7.3), so in the relational model the table has a single foreign key, toward SEGMENTO. It is not counted among the weak entities: its key is the full key of the pair, not a partial identifier within a parent.

5. Special attributes and weak entities

5.1 Derived attributes

They are not stored: they are computed from other data.

  • VUELO.duracion_estimada_total: sum of the durations of its segments.
  • SEGMENTO.retraso_salida_min: difference between the actual and the scheduled departure time.
  • INVENTARIO_PLAZAS.plazas_disponibles: total seats minus occupied seats.
  • CONEXION.tiempo_real_min: departure of the second leg minus arrival of the first.
  • CONEXION.es_misconnected: true when the actual time falls below the required minimum.

In the physical DDL, three of them are GENERATED ALWAYS AS … VIRTUAL columns (retraso_salida_min, plazas_disponibles and es_misconnected), so the database computes them from stored facts. The total flight duration is computed at query time from the segments; VUELO stores only an optional published estimate (duracion_estimada_min). tiempo_real_min spans two different rows, which a virtual column cannot reference, so it is stored once the connection has happened.

5.2 Multivalued attributes, resolved into entities

A multivalued attribute violates first normal form, so each one is extracted into its own entity:

  • Crew qualifications (“which models can this person fly”) → CALIFICACION, which also gains its own validity attributes.
  • Roles per segment → ASIGNACION_TRIPULACION. The role is an attribute of the relationship: the same person can be captain on one segment and first officer on another.
  • Passengers of a PNR → RESERVA_PASAJERO.
  • Runways of an airport → RUNWAY.

6. Constraints the E-R model cannot express

They are listed explicitly to record that they were identified and that leaving them out of the diagram is deliberate.

ConstraintWhy it cannot be represented, and how it is implemented
Origin ≠ destination in a segmentIt compares two attributes of the same row: implemented with CHECK (aeropuerto_origen <> aeropuerto_destino).
No identical segments within an operationAlready covered by the primary key, which includes num_secuencia.
A crew member cannot be on two overlapping segmentsIt requires comparing time intervals across different rows. It needs an exclusion constraint, a trigger or validation in the application.
An aircraft cannot operate overlapping segmentsSame case as the previous one, on the registration assigned to the segment.
Captain and first officer qualified for the modelIt crosses four entities: ASIGNACION → SEGMENTO → AERONAVE → MODELO, checked against a valid CALIFICACION.
The aircraft needs a long enough runwayIt compares the model's minimum length against the longest runway of the origin and destination airports.
Occupied seats ≤ total seatsCHECK inside INVENTARIO_PLAZAS; keeping the counter in sync with bookings requires a trigger.
The assigned seat belongs to the segment's aircraftGuaranteed with a composite foreign key if RESERVA_SEGMENTO carries the segment's registration.

Two constraints that did become declarative. The gate foreign keys include the airport code, (aeropuerto_origen, terminal_salida, gate_salida), so it is impossible to assign a gate located in MEX to a flight that departs from GUA.

And a UNIQUE (id_tripulante, tipo) on TRIPULANTE lets the subtypes reference that pair, which prevents registering a cabin crew member as a pilot.

7. Modeling alternatives considered

7.1 Composite natural keys vs. surrogate keys

The model uses natural keys: SEGMENTO is identified by num_vuelo + fecha_operacion + num_secuencia. That is correct at the conceptual level, because it expresses the identity dependency and makes the ordinality of the stops visible.

Cost: that three-attribute key propagates to ASIGNACION_TRIPULACION, RESERVA_SEGMENTO, INVENTARIO_PLAZAS, EVENTO_OPERATIVO and ALERTA_IRREGULARIDAD, which end up with very wide foreign keys.

Alternative: give OPERACION_VUELO and SEGMENTO a surrogate key and keep the natural key as a UNIQUE constraint. Joins become simpler without losing any rule, because uniqueness is still guaranteed. It is the option we would choose for the physical implementation.

7.2 Crew subtypes

PILOTO and TRIPULANTE_CABINA were modeled as subtypes. The alternative, a single entity with a discriminator and nullable columns, avoids two tables, but it leaves the license number and license type null for all cabin crew and makes it impossible to require them for pilots with a simple constraint.

7.3 Catalogs vs. closed domains

Operational status, crew role, cabin class, event type and delay reason were left as attributes with an enumerated domain, so as not to inflate the diagram with two-column entities. If the business needed to add values without altering tables, for example new delay reasons, each of them should become a catalog with its own key.

7.4 Connection as an entity vs. an attribute

CONEXION was modeled as an associative entity between two itinerary lines. The alternative would be a tiempo_conexion attribute on RESERVA_SEGMENTO, but then there would be nowhere to record the required minimum time or the misconnected flag, and the explicit relationship between the arriving leg and the departing leg would be lost.
